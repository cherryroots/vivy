# Game Boy

Vivy will play Game Boy games by herself: work out the controls and what the game wants, play in short capped sessions, and post now and then in a Discord thread about how far she has got. This is being built in stages, one pull request each. **Stages 1 to 3 are done: the emulator, played by hand with admin commands; code that reads Pokémon Red's screen and memory, keeps a map of each area and walks her anywhere on it; and a cheap model (the supervisor) that carries out one short task at a time, with the gate that measures how well it does. Stage 4 is under way: so far, skills she writes herself, and playing by herself in capped sessions with a journal, checkpoints and a stuck ladder. She doesn't post about it in her own voice yet (stage 5).**

The plan, with the research behind it and every decision, is the "Vivy plays any Game Boy game" doc in the project. The stages are at the bottom of this page.

## Playing by hand

There's one Game Boy for the whole bot. It only moves when someone presses something, or while she plays by herself (`play`, below); then the commands that move the game or change her skills are refused until she stops. Every command is for admins (`admins` in `config.toml`).

| Command | What it does |
|---|---|
| `/games gameboy load rom:` | Puts a ROM from `roms_dir` in and switches it on, with a GIF of the first 4 seconds. Whatever was in before is gone (save it first). |
| `/games gameboy press buttons:` | Presses buttons one after another and posts a GIF of what happened. `hold:` is how many frames each press is held (default 16, one step in most games that walk on a grid), `gap:` how many frames nothing is held after each (default 16). |
| `/games gameboy wait seconds:` | Lets the game run with nothing pressed (1 to 60 s, default 3), with a GIF. |
| `/games gameboy screen` | The screen as it is now, as a PNG, with how long it has been played. |
| `/games gameboy gif seconds:` | The last few seconds (at most 10) as a GIF. |
| `/games gameboy look` | What code reads from the game: where she is, the facts from its memory (badges, money, party, bag, story so far), the map of the area so far, the words on screen and the last 5 steps. In other games code reads them with no memory map (below): where she is, the area and the map, but no facts or words yet. |
| `/games gameboy walk x: y:` | Walks to that step of the map (code finds the way and takes it), with a GIF and the `look` after. Leave out `x` and `y` to walk to the nearest step not seen yet. Stops early when she walks into another area or something takes over the screen, like a text box. |
| `/games gameboy task task: actions:` | A cheap model (the supervisor, below) carries out one short task by itself, like `go down the stairs, until you're on the floor below`, taking at most `actions:` actions (default 30). Posts whether it says it's done, its last actions, a GIF of the last seconds and the `look` after. Pokémon Red and Blue only for now. |
| `/games gameboy skills forget:` | The skills she has written for the game that's in (below), each with when it's for, its notes, its rules and how its last use went. `forget:` deletes one by name. |
| `/games gameboy play minutes: goal: episodes:` | She plays by herself from where the game is, in the background, for up to `minutes:` (default `session_minutes`, 60) or `episodes:` short tasks (200), until the day's money for playing is spent, or until she's stuck for good (below). `goal:` replaces her goal for this game and is kept (default: play through to the credits). The reply is the dashboard (below), kept up to date while she plays. A thread under it is the log: a message per episode, a picture at each checkpoint, a warning when she's stuck, a 💀 line when she blacks out, and the summary with a GIF of the last seconds at the end, so it reads in order afterwards (in a thread already, or without permission to start one, they go in the channel). |
| `/games gameboy stop` | Stops her playing, at once; the summary follows. |
| `/games gameboy status` | Her goal, milestones, notes and last episodes for the game that's in, whether she's playing, and what playing used today (paid dollars and free tokens). |
| `/games gameboy dashboard` | Posts the dashboard here. While she plays, it moves: the old one then links to the new one. Otherwise it shows how her last session left things. |
| `/games gameboy save name:` | Saves the game exactly as it is, under a name like `before Brock`. |
| `/games gameboy restore name:` | Goes back to a save of the ROM that's in. |
| `/games gameboy off` | Takes the ROM out. |

What to press is typed as words, with spaces or commas between them:

- a button: `a`, `b`, `start`, `select`, `up`, `down`, `left`, `right` (or `u`, `d`, `l`, `r`)
- two at once: `a+b`
- a repeat: `up*3` (or `upx3`)
- held longer (or shorter) than the usual press: `a:40`, in frames from 1 to 120 (`a:40*2` twice)
- a pause as long as a press: `wait` (`wait:60` for a second)

So `start wait a a up*3 a` presses Start, waits, presses A twice, Up three times and A. One command takes at most 64 presses.

## ROMs and saves

ROMs are `.gb` and `.gbc` files in `roms_dir` (default `roms/`, next to the bot). They have to be your own copies of games you own; the bot never downloads any. Game Boy Color games run on a Game Boy Color, the rest on an original Game Boy. Game Boy Advance games don't run (that's stage 7).

Save states go in `saves_dir` (default `gameboy/`), a folder per ROM named after its file: `gameboy/red/before Brock.state`. A state is the whole machine, about 50 KB, so it only loads into the same ROM. A save the game makes itself (the SAVE menu in Pokémon) lives in the cartridge's memory, which a state includes, so saving a state after it keeps it. Her skills for a game are in the same folder, as `skills.json`. Going back to a save keeps them, since they're what she knows rather than where the game is. Both folders are in `.gitignore`.

## How it works

`boytacean` (Apache-2.0, pinned to exactly 0.13.2, since save states may not load in another version) emulates the Game Boy and Game Boy Color in Rust, headless. It starts each game with its own open-source boot ROM, so no Nintendo boot ROM is needed. On Cherry's Pokémon Red it runs about 60 times real time on one core in a release build, with sound off.

- **One frame at a time.** `Emulator::frame` runs one frame (1/60 s of game time) and stops at the end of V-Blank, the point where the next frame starts. Saving a state anywhere else, for example right as V-Blank starts while the game's V-Blank interrupt is pending, loses that interrupt, and the game drifts from the one that saved it. Stopping there every time makes every save safe, and a loaded state plays back the same inputs frame for frame (tested on Red).
- **Its own thread.** `console.rs` runs the one `Machine` on a thread of its own and hands it jobs: `console().with(|machine| ...)` sends a closure there and waits for the answer, like `ctx.db.call` for the database. Emulating never holds up the async tasks. If the emulator panics, that job fails ("the Game Boy crashed... and was turned off"), the machine is taken out, and the thread carries on.
- **GIFs.** The machine keeps a screen every 3 frames (20 a second) for the last 10 seconds. A Game Boy shows 4 shades and a Game Boy Color at most 56 colours at once, so a GIF's 256-colour palette holds them exactly, without dithering. Screens the same as the one before are folded into one longer frame, so a GIF of 10 seconds of Red is about 150 KB. GIFs are twice the screen's size (320×288) and PNGs three times (480×432).
- `boytacean` prints `[WARNING] Writing to unknown Cartridge ROM location 0x6000` with `println!` (not the log) every time Pokémon Red touches its cartridge's clock latch, which Red's cartridge doesn't have. It's harmless but floods the journal, so once the Game Boy starts, `core::logging::drop_prints` points stdout at `/dev/null`. Log lines don't go through stdout itself: they use a copy of it taken at start.

## Reading the game (stage 2)

Code reads everything it can from the Game Boy itself, so a model is only asked what needs judgement. Nothing here calls a model.

- **The screen from video memory** (`screen.rs`). Each of the 20×18 cells comes from the background or the window layer, the way the Game Boy picks (LCDC bits, signed tile numbers, scrolling that wraps at the edge), plus the sprites. Each tile also gets a fingerprint of its pixels (FNV-1a, so it never changes between builds). A test redraws the screen from the cells and matches the real one pixel for pixel. The emulator copies video memory as V-Blank starts (LY 144), when the frame is finished.
- **Fades and area changes** (`change.rs`). A frame is a picture, off, or blank (every pixel one colour, or a palette that maps every colour to one shade). A dark spell of at least 4 frames is an area change; Red's doors are dark for about 18.
- **Text** (`text.rs`). Font tiles are labelled by fingerprint, so text reads exactly once each letter is known. A tile given two different labels reads as neither. In Red the tile numbers are the character codes, so `Font::learn_from_numbers` labels them; a test checks the font read from video memory gives the same words as Red's memory. Other games will need a model to label each new letter once (stage 7).
- **Steps** (`moves.rs`). A step holds the direction until the screen starts to scroll (at most 24 frames), lets go and waits for the screen to be still. A 16-pixel scroll is a step, 32 a ledge jump, no scroll with a changed player sprite a turn, nothing a block, a dark spell somewhere new. Walking tries again after a turn, since the first press only turns her.
- **Pokémon Red's memory map** (`red/`). Addresses from the pret/pokered disassembly: map and position, facing, battle, names, badges, money (decimal digits), party (species, level, HP, status), bag, doors and signs, the tileset's list of tiles that can be walked on, tall grass, 33 story flags in the order the game reaches them ("chose a starter", "beat Brock", ...), and in a battle both Pokémon (species, level, HP, status) and her moves with the PP left, once both are out (until then the battle copies hold the last battle's). Names of species, items and maps are tables made from the disassembly. `State::facts` writes them as prompt lines. `Trust` checks memory against the screen: each step, the scroll and the position in memory must agree; after 3 agreements memory is trusted, and after one disagreement its facts are left out for good (a Japanese cartridge or a hack could keep things elsewhere).
- **Looking at Red** (`red/look.rs`). Red keeps its own copy of the screen's tiles and checks one tile per step against the walkable list, so code marks every step on screen as floor, wall, tall grass, door (from the doors in memory), sign, or edge (past the map's edge where the next area joins) exactly the way the game decides. People come from the sprites. A text box or menu is open when its corner tiles are on screen. She can only walk on the map itself: every tile a map tile (so not the Pokédex page or the swirl into a battle), her sprite in the middle, standing on a walkable tile, and no scene running (Red's `wJoyIgnore` and its scripted-movement flags), so nothing else is ever marked on the map.
- **Area maps** (`map.rs`). A map per area that fills in as she goes: what each step is, steps she walked onto (always floor, whatever they look like), steps that blocked her from one side, doors and where they led, where a step took her when it wasn't the next step, her own markers, and the people on the last screen (not saved, they move). `path` finds the shortest way (breadth-first), through unseen steps only when nothing known reaches, and never through unseen steps past the area's edges when the game gives its size (Red does), since that wanders around the outside of the map; doors and edges can only be the last step, since stepping on one leaves the area. Two kinds of step are learned by trying them, in any game: a **one-way step** (`↓` and the other arrows on the map text), when a press took her two steps on, hopping over the step between (Red's ledges): the way goes over it its way in one press and around it the other way; and a **step that carries her** (`*`), when a press left her anywhere else (floor arrows, a spinner, ice): the way uses where that step took her before, and from anywhere else such a step can only be the last one, since nobody knows yet where it goes. Both stay whatever the step looks like afterwards (Red's look calls a ledge a wall), and a plan from the puzzle helper can't end on a one-way step. `frontier` is the unseen steps next to what she can reach, nearest first. `draw` writes it as text with x along the top and y down the side, with a legend.
- **Walking** (`walk.rs`). `walk_to` finds the way, takes one step, looks again, and repeats. A step that didn't move her marks that way as blocked (unless someone was standing there, or something took over the screen) and the way is found again. The way found is kept while its next step is still open: found again at every step, people walking about made the shortest way flip between two, and she went back and forth for 60 steps in Link's Awakening's village. It stops when she arrives, walks into another area (waiting for the fade to finish, since Red changes its map number about 20 frames before fading), something takes over the screen (it waits up to 2 seconds for the words), there's no way, or 6 steps failed. `explore` walks toward the nearest unseen step until it has been seen. Every step goes in the movement log (`world.rs`), as lines like `Pallet Town (5, 6) up: moved to (5, 5)`.

## Short tasks (stage 3)

The supervisor carries out one short task at a time, like "take the Potion out of the PC in your room, until it's in your bag". It's the `game_play` job: Claude Haiku at low effort with no web or code, unless `[ai.claude.jobs.game_play]` says otherwise. Its prompt names no game and stays the same for every task, so it's cached; the game and the task come in the first message, with where she is and a picture of the screen.

- **Actions** (`actions.rs`). The model picks one tool at a time and code does the work: `walk_to x y` (walking as in stage 2), `explore`, `press` (1 to 5 buttons, like `up a`, and buttons joined with + held together, like `b+right` for a step with B held: the tool now says so, since in session 17 she never held Link's shield up while walking), `read_text` (presses A until the text is gone or a choice comes up, at most 30 times), `write_skill` and `use_skill` (stage 4, below), and `finish done summary`. After each one the screen settles first: she only counts as free to walk if she still is a third of a second later (the rival walks up a moment after his words close), and typed-out text is waited for until it stops changing. `walk_to` a step outside the area (when its size is known) is refused with the area's size and how to leave by an edge, unless it's an edge step with no wall inside it: those are the ways out. Exploring never aims past the edges, and never tries the same unreachable step twice, so it always ends. Finishing as done while text is still up gets one "read it first" back, since what the game does after the text can be what finishes the task (the rival battle is only saved after its last words). The answer is one line of what happened, then where she is (with the area's size): the facts from memory, whether she can walk, the words on screen and the map 7 steps around her, plus the screen at twice its size (about 120 tokens). Words only count inside a box, so the swirl into a battle doesn't read as letters.
- **The loop** (`supervisor.rs`). `run_task` asks for an action, runs it, sends back what happened, and repeats until `finish` or the action limit. Answering in words gets two reminders to use a tool; three actions in a row that change nothing get a warning to check which way she's facing and try something else. The `Episode` keeps every action and its outcome and all the words seen, and `digest` writes that for the strategist. Code, not the model's `finish`, decides whether a task was really done.
- **Where it plays** (`player.rs`). The supervisor plays through the `Player` trait: in the bot that's the one Game Boy on its thread; the gate has a Game Boy of its own.
- **The strategist** (`strategist.rs`). Once per episode a stronger OpenAI model reads the goal, its notes, the last episode's digest and where she is, and answers with up to 15 notes and the next short task, as JSON that fits a strict schema (`OpenAi::structured`, nothing stored). It's asked in one call, or in two (notes first, then the task with the new notes). The gate measures which works; the bot uses it from stage 4.

## The gate

Before anything is built on the supervisor, stage 3 measures it on 15 tasks from Red's opening, each run 3 times. The gate passes when at least half the runs are done. If Haiku stays under that, the plan's fallbacks are tried in cost order: Haiku with hints from the strategist, a model from OpenAI's free group playing, Gemini 3.8 Flash playing, Sonnet playing.

The tasks start from save states that `build_the_task_states` makes by playing a new game by script (walking with code, mashing through text and battles), from her bedroom to Viridian City with Oak's Parcel: `bedroom`, `downstairs`, `pallet`, `lab`, `starter`, `after_rival`, `pallet_starter`, `route1`, `viridian` and `parcel`. Each task is checked in code from the game's memory and the words seen: the area she ends in, the bag, the party, a story flag, or words like "Shades of" from the town sign. They are going downstairs, the Potion from the PC, talking to Mom, going outside, reading the town sign, heading north until Oak stops her, choosing a starter, the rival battle, leaving the lab, onto Route 1, up Route 1 to Viridian City (with wild Pokémon in the grass), into the Mart for the parcel, healing at the Pokémon Center, saving the game, and leaving the Mart. `actions_carry_out_tasks_by_script` checks the actions and the checks with no model, on five of them.

The runs are `#[ignore]`d tests that only call models with `VIVY_GB_GATE=1`. They need your ROM, the keys (`VIVY_ANTHROPIC_API_KEY` or `ANTHROPIC_API_KEY`, `OPENAI_API_KEY`, `GEMINI_API_KEY`) and the states (in `VIVY_GB_TASKS`, default the project's shared folder):

```bash
export VIVY_GB_ROM=roms/red.gb VIVY_GB_GATE=1
cargo test --release build_the_task_states -- --ignored     # the states, once
cargo test --release gate_keys_work -- --ignored --nocapture # one small call to each
cargo test --release gate_tasks -- --ignored --nocapture     # the supervisor on the tasks
cargo test --release gate_strategists -- --ignored --nocapture
```

`gate_tasks` takes `VIVY_GB_PLAYER` (the model playing, default `claude-haiku-5-5`; `gemini-...` and `gpt-...` models work too), `VIVY_GB_EFFORT` (`low`), `VIVY_GB_RUNS` (3), `VIVY_GB_ONLY` (only tasks whose names contain it) and `VIVY_GB_HINTS` (an OpenAI model that writes a hint before each task). `gate_strategists` plays the long goal, bringing Oak's Parcel back to Oak, from `parcel` with each model in `VIVY_GB_STRATEGISTS` (`gpt-6-astra,gpt-6.1-sol`) asked in one call and in two: at most 15 episodes of 30 actions. Each run stops at `VIVY_GB_GATE_USD` dollars (15) at list prices, and writes `report.md`, `log.md` (every episode's actions and words) and each run's last screen to a new folder in `VIVY_GB_RESULTS` (default `/mnt/project-files/gameboy/gate`). A cloud session's proxy re-signs TLS, so the gate also trusts the certificate in `SSL_CERT_FILE`.

**Results (20 October 2026).** Haiku 5.5 at low effort passed: 43 of 45 runs done (96%), never claiming a task done when it wasn't, for $0.10 in all (about $0.002 a task). Every task went 3 for 3 except up Route 1 (1 of 3): both misses used up their 50 actions in wild battles, since she reads each battle turn as separate actions. On the long goal, Astra and 6.1 Sol both got the parcel to Oak in all four runs (5 or 6 episodes, 64 to 77 actions), planning the same five legs; two calls doubled the strategist's tokens for no gain, so the strategist is asked in one. None of the fallbacks were needed. The reports are in the project's shared folder under `gameboy/gate/`.

## Her own skills (stage 4)

The gate's one weak spot was battles: she plays each turn as separate actions, so wild battles on Route 1 ate her action budget. Coding a battle action would fix that for Red only, and it would be code playing for her. Instead she writes skills herself (`skills.rs`), and code only follows them.

- **What a skill is.** A name, a line saying when it's for, and know-how, a routine, or both. Know-how is notes she reads when she uses the skill (how a puzzle works, which moves are strong). A routine is up to 8 rules, each "when these words are on screen, press these 1 to 5 buttons" (whole words, any case: `RED` isn't in "appeared"; only marks, like the cursor `▶`, are looked for as they are) (the first rule that fits wins; one without words fits any screen), and when to stop: when she can walk (unless she says not to), when any of her stop words is on screen, after a number of rounds (30 unless she says, at most 50), when no rule fits, or when 3 rounds in a row change nothing. Stop words only count after its first press, so words already up when it starts (`RUN` on the battle menu it's meant for) don't stop it at once, and `write_skill` warns when a stop word is also a rule's words.
- **Nothing in code knows what a skill is for.** Rules and stops only use what every game has, the words on screen and whether she can walk, so a battle routine in Red is hers, and in another game she writes others. `skills_she_writes_carry_out_battles` runs three a model might write on the real game: pressing A through the rival battle, running from a wild Pokémon (`down right a` on the battle menu) and fighting one.
- **Writing and using them.** `write_skill` saves one (the same name replaces it, and its record starts over); `use_skill` shows its notes and runs its routine as one action, answering with the presses, the rounds, why it stopped and the words it went through. When no rule fits, it says what the screen says; when it pressed nothing, it says to rewrite it under the same name, so she fixes a skill instead of writing near-copies beside it. Each skill keeps how many times it was used and how the last use ended. Her list of skills (name, when it's for, how the last use went) comes with every look, and the prompt asks her to write one when she catches herself repeating presses, and to rewrite one that didn't do what she meant. At most 20 per game.
- **Astra reviews them.** The supervisor (Haiku) is good at noticing a repeated job and drafting a skill on the spot, and bad at editing them. So the strategist's one call per episode (same model, Cherry's call) also gets her skills in full, with how each last use went, and answers with changes (`skill_changes` in its schema: rewrite or delete, with `write_skill`'s fields). It fixes a skill whose last use went wrong, merges near-copies, deletes ones that never helped and adds one for a repeated job no skill covers; an empty list keeps them all. `skills::review` applies the changes, checked like any skill she writes.
- **Where they're kept.** `skills.json` next to the game's save states, read when the ROM is loaded and written after each `task` that changed them (even one that failed partway). A file that doesn't read is logged and counts as none.

`gate_learning` measures whether she gets better by writing them: the rival battle, then up Route 1, 5 times in a row (`VIVY_GB_RUNS`), each run starting from the same state but with the skills written in the runs before. Its report has each run's actions and the skills written and used, and her skills at the end (also as `skills.json`). `VIVY_GB_LEARN` names other tasks, and `VIVY_GB_REVIEWER` (an OpenAI model, like `gpt-6-astra`) has it review her skills after each task, to compare runs with and without the review.

**First run (20 October 2026, before the fixes above).** The rival battle was done 4 of 5 times, up Route 1 2 of 5 ($0.06 in all). She wrote skills in four runs without being told how, and a wild battle went from about 10 single presses to 2 skill uses. Route 1's misses came from walking, not fighting: `walk_to` targets past the top edge wandered around the outside of the map. She also finished once with the battle's last words still up, wasted skills whose stop word (`RUN`) was on the very menu they were for, never learned why a skill matched nothing, and wrote 5 near-copies rather than fixing one. The fixes since are the refused targets and bounded paths, the "read it first" guard, stop words after the first press with a warning, the screen's words in "no rule fits", and a nudge to rewrite under the same name. (A press like `aa` now says to put spaces between them.) The same day's baseline with no skills did up Route 1 3 of 3 in 45 actions each.

**With the fixes, and with the review (20 October 2026).** Without the review she did all 10 tasks (up from 6), ending with 2 battle skills and no near-copies. With Astra reviewing after each task she did 9 of 10; the miss was Claude overloaded before her first action, which the gate now plays again once. Astra changed her skills twice in 10 reviews, merging them into one battle skill that won the rival battle in one action, and it dropped a stop word, `RED`, that had stopped every wild battle after one press. That was a matching bug, `RED` inside "appeared", now fixed by matching whole words. Haiku cost about $0.03 a gate run; each review about $0.03 at list prices, on the free daily tokens. Up Route 1's action counts mostly show whether the same path from the same state meets a wild Pokémon, so they say little about skills; longer play will. That run also found exploring could loop forever at the top of a route, now fixed.

## Holding buttons, what's near her, and reflexes

Fights and jumps happen in real time, while a model call between presses takes seconds of game time (the game waits, but she sees only one screen per call). Three things cover it, none knowing a game:

- **Holding buttons.** `walk_to`, `explore` and `press` take `holding`, buttons kept down the whole action, gaps and steps included (`Emulator::keep`), like `b` for Link's shield while he walks. Held, his shield pushes a sea urchin out of the way and takes no hit; without it the same walk costs half a heart. A press can also be longer than a step: `a:40` holds A for 40 frames (up to 120), for a charged shot or a higher jump, and `b+right` presses both together.
- **What's near her** (`near.rs`). Every look she can walk in ends with a line like `Near you (steps from you): 1 right 2 up, still; 2 left, moving down, coming closer.`: the other sprites within 4 steps, nearest first, parts that touch taken as one thing, and how each moved from her over the last fifth of a second (`Machine::sprites_since`). Seen from her, so a person standing still comes closer as she walks up to them.
- **Reflexes.** A skill rule can take `if_near`, what must be around her: `near`, `ahead` (the way she faces), `behind`, `above`, `below`, `left`, `right` or `coming` (moving closer), then 1 to 4 steps; or `blocked`, when her last direction didn't move her. A skill with such a rule is a reflex: code runs it in real time while she walks, looking after each press with no waiting for the screen to settle and only a 4-frame gap between presses, so she reacts as things move with no model call in between. It stops when words come up, when she can't walk for 3 rounds running (a moment of camera catch-up or knockback doesn't stop it), when no rule fits, or after its rounds. A skill's `holding` keeps buttons down while it runs. She writes reflexes herself, like any skill; the prompt says when they help.

Mega Man V had been unplayable: his sprites were never seen, since the game turns sprites off and the scroll back to nothing for the status bar at the bottom, and by V-Blank, where the picture was read, only the bar's settings were left. The picture is now read with the LCDC and scroll most of its lines were drawn with (`Emulator::frame` keeps each line's), and rows drawn with others are a status bar: read with their own settings and kept where they are, like the window, when the map scrolls (the camera following him had changed 19 of the bar's cells and stopped him). Now code finds Mega Man by pressing, and one reflex written by hand (`blocked` → `a:24 right:16`, `ahead 4` → `b`, otherwise `right`) ran, jumped onto a ledge and shot at what came for 31 rounds before he fell, with no model call. Sample: `gameboy/samples/megaman-reflex.gif` in the project's shared folder.

## Her own cheat finder

Code reads Red's memory through a map made from the disassembly, and finds where any other game keeps her position by itself. Everything else a game keeps (HP, money, a counter in a puzzle) she can find herself, the way cheat finders do it (`ram.rs`). Code only keeps the candidates and reads the bytes; when to search and what for is hers, and the strategist can make it a task.

- **`ram_search`.** `start` begins with every byte of work RAM (C000 to DFFF) and high RAM (FF80 to FFFE) read five ways: one byte, two bytes either way round (`le16`, `be16`), and two or three bytes of decimal digits (`bcd2`, `bcd3`, as Red keeps money). Then she does something in the game and says what the number did since the last search: `changed`, `same`, `up`, `down`, or `equals` a value she can see on screen (which starts a search by itself). Each round keeps what did that and answers with how many are left, listing up to 8 as `D347 bcd3 = 3175`.
- **`ram_watch`.** Names an address and format, like `money`, `D347`, `bcd3`. Watched values are a line of the facts after every action (`Watched: money 3175`), at most 8. The same name replaces one; an empty address stops it. They're kept in `watches.json` next to her skills, read when the ROM is loaded and written after each `task` that changed them and with each session's saves.
- **On Red** (`a_search_finds_reds_money_and_her_x`, needs the ROM): `equals` with the money on screen finds `D347 bcd3` (Red's `wPlayerMoney`) in one round; walking left and right keeps Red's x (`D362`) among the few hundred bytes that move with her (her sprite and the screen keep copies), which is why position stays code's job.

## Playing by herself (stage 4)

`/games gameboy play` hands her the game (`session.rs`). Each episode the strategist reads her journal and picks one short task, the supervisor carries it out in at most 30 actions, and code counts what was new. She plays from wherever the game is, so `restore latest` first carries on from the last session.

- **Who plans.** The strategist is the `game_plan` job on OpenAI's own endpoint (`OPENAI_TOKEN`), on the free daily data-sharing tokens. It's GPT-6 Astra unless `[ai.openai.jobs.game_plan] model` says otherwise (`gpt-6.1-sol` is the other free choice), and its reviews are the same model at high effort (`game_review`). - **Her journal.** `journal.json` next to her skills: the goal, the strategist's notes as it last wrote them, her milestones, a line per episode (the last 200), what the paid models have cost on this game ever, and what code has counted. - **Notes with sources** (`notes.rs`). Each note says where it came from: `seen` (on screen or in the facts), `tried` (a task showed it), `hint from <name>` (someone watching), or a `guess`. A way in that's floor isn't counted either way, since a door is hard to tell from the step in front of it. Notes about what changes by itself (HP, levels, money, where she stands) are marked as such, since the facts already show them as they are now. One with neither is dropped, and an answer without any keeps the ones there are. Being in an area from before doesn't count, so "Bring the parcel back to Oak" isn't reached by having met him once, and a milestone with the same test as one ahead is dropped as a repeat. What she claims doesn't count. The first finished episode after progress doesn't count as stuck even with nothing new, since walking back out of a building she just went into finds nothing new by nature.
- **Tasks.** The strategist keeps a task to one area (or from it into the next), since wild battles use up the 30 actions, and a task to reach an area ends "until you're in" its name. Code checks that one: finishing somewhere else gets her told where she is, once. Any other task finished in an area it didn't start in or name gets her asked, once, whether something sent her there (in a real session she lost to a Bug Catcher, woke up in Pallet Town and called the battle done). Several actions in one answer also stop at 30. Stop words in her skills can't have numbers in them, since HP, levels and money change.

Nothing in it knows a game: the mechanics come from her own movement log and notes.

- **Nothing lost.** After every episode the journal, the maps (`maps.json`), her skills, the day's use and a save called `latest` are written (each through a temporary file), so a restart loses one episode at most. The Timers box on the status picture shows "Game Boy" while she plays, and her calls show on the spend card as `game_play` (and `game_plan` on OpenAI).

## Reading a game with no memory map (stage 6)

Every game but Pokémon Red and Blue is looked at by `any::look` (`walk::look_for` picks it from the cartridge's title), which learns where the game keeps things the way cheat finders do, and what the screen's steps are by trying them. Nothing in it knows Red: `VIVY_GB_LOOK=any` makes the gate's player look at Red this way too, to measure it.

- **Where she stands** (`any/memory.rs`). Each step's scroll says how far she moved. Code copies work and high RAM (8,319 bytes) before and after, and every byte starts as a guess at her x or y, counted in steps or in 2, 4, 8 or 16 pixels a step. A byte that didn't change by what the step did is a miss; one miss is let off, and one more for every 8 steps it got right, since walking into the next area makes the real one jump. A byte that followed 4 steps along its axis is picked, and kept while it stays in the running. Until then code counts steps itself, and once found, what it drew moves to the game's numbers. A byte that counts pixels comes round every 16 steps, so on a map wider than that (Harvest Moon's farm, where the screen scrolls under her) her x jumped 16 steps back each time, and walking to the far side made a new place. So once a byte is picked, a second vote finds the byte that counts it going round: it goes up by one on the step the picked byte goes past 255, down by one on the step it goes below 0, and never changes otherwise. Every byte starts as a guess, one that changed when it shouldn't have before the first time round is out at once, and one that went round with it and stayed put for 8 steps besides is found (in Harvest Moon, C607 beside x's C606). From then on her x is the two together, and what was drawn of the place moves by the 16 steps each round adds (`Byte::high`, saved with the byte). On a walk along the farm and back, before, her x jumped from 10 to 1 when the bytes were found and stepping left past 0 made a new place at 15; now it's one place, and every look after the bytes are found is the same steps from the game's own position.
- **Free to walk.** Her sprite is the one nearest the middle of the screen, no window covers her, and at most 8 tiles changed since the last calm screen (shifted by the scroll); a tile counts as the same if its number or its picture is. A scroll with no step taken and no direction pressed means the game is moving her (a scene); one she pressed herself is her walking. With no calm screen to compare with (after a fade, and all through a battle), the screen must hold still for 20 frames and a sprite in the middle must be one of hers: code keeps the pictures of the sprites in the middle whenever a press of hers scrolls the screen and she's free to walk, so a battle's pictures in the middle (the other Pokémon sliding in, her back) aren't her, even when she pressed a direction in its menus, and a battle is never a place. A scroll of more than two steps leaves nothing to compare, so from then until she's free a sprite in the middle must be hers too.
- **What each step is.** A step's picture is its four tiles' fingerprints together. Code remembers what trying one did: walked onto it (floor), blocked by it with nobody there and still free to walk (wall; a scene that stops her, like Oak calling out in Pallet Town, says nothing about the step), went somewhere new on it through a fade (door), hopped over it (one way), carried on past it (carries her). A picture walked onto more often than it went somewhere is floor: an edge (somewhere new with no fade) teaches its picture nothing, since it's ground like any other, which had made Viridian City a sea of doors she couldn't walk through. Every step on screen with a known picture is marked on the map, so one bump teaches every wall that looks the same, and one hop every ledge.
- **Somewhere new.** A step that put her somewhere memory says the step couldn't have, or that went dark and left her free to walk, is a new place, even when the numbers are the ones the step would give (Red's stairs land on the same spot a floor down, in a room that looks much like the one above). The swirl into a battle goes dark too, but she isn't free after it. With no step taken, a screen quite unlike where she last walked is somewhere new too (a scene moved her, a checkpoint loaded), and so is a fade since the last look she could walk at, while her map still showed at every look in between (a menu or a text box over it is fine): she went through a door by her own presses (Red's stairs, which land in a room much like hers; a battle ends dark too, but its own screen was up). Quite unlike means fewer than half the steps seen there before look the same, or almost none of its pictures were ever seen there while the steps seen before don't vouch for it; a few steps changing says nothing, since flowers and water move. Pictures seen to change on a step while she stood still, with its tiles keeping their numbers and the same change seen twice (flowers, water), are left out when telling places apart; a looser rule once learned most of a town's pictures as moving in a live run, and every place then looked like every other. Arrivals are only looked at once she can walk, since a text box can cover them.
- **Which place it is.** A byte that changes at every new place and hardly ever while she walks around inside one says the area. Places are "Place 1", "Place 2" and so on, in the order she finds them, and keep those names once the byte is found, since her notes use them: the byte only tells places apart (one she's been to has the value it had then). Until it's found, a place she's been is known again by its pictures, never the one she just left (Red's stairs land in a room that looks much like the one above), and that counts as going back through a door. The pictures that cover a fifth of the screen or more (plain floor, the dark past the walls) must not be all that matches: when at least 4 of the others were seen there too, they must look the same as well, since rooms built alike share their floor at the same steps (a Route 2 gate once matched her house). Nor does the byte ever name the place she just left. Code keeps every byte's tally and takes the one that changed at the most new places and stayed put at the fewest, then changed least while walking, then took the most values. A byte that stays put as she goes somewhere new would give two places one name, and one that changes while she walks around isn't the area, so either is dropped as the pick at once. Going back through a door drops the bytes that don't hold what they held when she first came through it (a byte that keeps the last area), and a place that looks different drops the bytes that hold what they held in a place she's been (the people loaded, the music). Before two places get one name, their pictures are compared, and if they look different the byte goes. Places that turn out to be one are merged under the older name.
- **Saved** in `sight.json` next to the maps (the bytes found and every picture learned), so a restart doesn't learn them again.

- **Games where she walks across the screen.** In Red the screen scrolls under her and her sprite stays in the middle. Many games keep the screen still and move her sprite instead (Link's Awakening's rooms, Harvest Moon's house), and scroll only near an edge. With no sprite in the middle and none of hers known yet, she isn't free to walk, and walking first finds her the way a cheat finder narrows down bytes (`moves::which_is_her`): it first waits for the screen to be drawn and still with sprites on it (a door's fade may still be going), then nothing is pressed for a moment, then each way in turn (each side of the way she last went, then on and back, so she ends about where she began: pressing back first walked her straight out of Link's Awakening's telephone hut, and pressing on first walked her back into Tarin's door, where he stops her, 350 times), every sprite is followed frame by frame, and only a sprite that stood still while nothing was pressed and went the way pressed whenever it moved, at least twice, is her. Children and animals wandering about move on their own or go some other way sooner or later (in Link's Awakening's village a fox walking past had been taken for Link, and she never got free; later a chicken pecking about was). If that leaves more than one, or none, it goes round once more. Each way and back can still leave her a little off where she began (Link ended 10 pixels up in the village), so the steps her sprite went from before the first press to the end are added to where she is: before, the next step was counted on from where she had been. Sprites that all did as pressed within a step and a half of each other are her together (Link holding something over his head). If that leaves no one sprite, walking takes up to 4 probe steps (toward where she's going first, then each way round) until one moves her, and a sprite that moved her way is only taken as hers once it does it twice running. The tools tell her when code doesn't know her yet, rather than blaming text that isn't there (a live run spent 4 episodes pressing into a corner, sure a text box was up). From then on her own sprites are followed one by one, those in a 16-pixel square around her (`moves::Parts`), not everything touching her, since a child standing right beside her is somebody else; her spot is their middle, wherever they go, so it stays on her through a screen sliding on to the next room, and a sprite there is her whichever way she faces. If no sprite is where she's thought to be, she's looked for by her pictures, and failing that found again by pressing; once her sprite has been known, not knowing it always counts as lost, and that's saved with where she stood (a save state after a game over had her neither found nor lost, and she couldn't walk for a whole episode). Steps are still 16 pixels: a game that moves her only while the button is down gets it held until she has gone 16, let go a little early by how far she drifts after letting go, and a step that stops short or goes a little past is finished or walked back. A hop or a carry is only learned where she's known to be, by memory or her sprite: the steps taken before Link's sprite was found had drawn a fresh village map as spinners. One pressed against a wall part way stays there, since walls sit on the game's own grid, so the first bump lines her steps up with it. A camera that stops part way through a tile (Harvest Moon scrolls 4 pixels at a time) is read in whole map tiles wherever the scroll is (`Screen::read_steady`). In these games memory isn't asked for her step: her steps are counted from her sprite (or from memory's pixels, below), since a byte that counts steps from pixels wraps every 16 steps and misses the part steps against walls, and a screen sliding on to the next room is the scroll less how far her sprite was carried back across the screen (a step or two), never a ledge. Her own presses (the press tool, not a step code takes) are counted too, and so is a screen still sliding on after them with nothing pressed since (a slide often ends a look or two after the press, and its end had been lost, a step or more each time): every frame's sprites are kept for the last 10 seconds (`Machine::sprites_since`), her sprites are followed through them from the last look (`moves::followed`), and the steps her sprite went, with the scroll, are added to where she is. Before, a press that moved her 2 steps facing a new way lost her sprite, and a step code took after it counted on from the wrong place, which is how her position drifted from the picture in session 4. A step code takes is counted the same way, on the screen's own grid from where her sprite was and is, so what she sees is marked where she stands, as long as that's the way pressed (a hop, or something carrying her straight on) and about as far as the step saw her go: otherwise the sprite followed wasn't her (a crab on Link's Awakening's beach, as the screen slid on, counted a step right as 7 steps left), so it counts as one step and she's looked for again by her pictures. A whole tile or more of scroll in one frame is another screen put up (a game over), not walking; and a step that slid the screen on to the next room isn't walked back to the end of a step, since back over the edge the screen slid back again and the step counted as 7. Through a door's fade she comes in somewhere else on the screen, so her spot is looked for again by her pictures (it had stayed on the telephone in the hut, and the hut never became a place), and where she was counts until her sprite is followed again. A fade seen while she couldn't walk yet (the room still coming in) counts as a door once she can. A step aside or a short hop is never taken as something carrying her on, since games like Link's Awakening nudge her round corners. As with a step code takes, only a door (a fade) takes her somewhere new by her own presses: a screen sliding on is the same place, since a screen looking unlike what was seen there split Link's Awakening's village into five places in session 5. Where she stood is saved with what code learned (`Sight`'s whereabouts), so a new session from `latest.state`, a replay and a rollback start on her step in her place, not at a new place's (0, 0). A place she comes back to is known by its pictures wherever she counted from: each picture seen there before suggests a shift, and the most suggested are tried. Through a door she went through before, only the place it led to is tried, and if its look doesn't settle it she starts on the step she came in on last time (`Sight`'s `came_in`): before, the steps she counted outside carried on inside, and session 9 drew Link's Awakening's outdoors into a house. Where the screen went dark while she couldn't walk (a game over, a scene) and what she sees now looks unlike where she was (her map merely not showing isn't enough: a slide changes the whole screen, and session 10 split the outdoors into eight places), she's somewhere else but not through a door: no door is marked where she stood, and the place she was in is tried too, at any shift (after a game over Link came back in Tarin's house, still counted on the beach). A fade still waiting for her to walk where a door led is no door once the screen goes dark again before she can: dying fades out, the game over menu comes up and the game starts her again somewhere, and in session 14 each game over had counted as a door, so the village outdoors became four places; replaying episode 483 from its save now keeps her in the village, found again by its look.

- **Her sprite's spot from memory** (`any/memory.rs`, `Pixels`). Following her sprite goes wrong when someone stands close or the screen slides on. So, in games where she walks across the screen, code also looks for the bytes the game draws her sprite from: a byte whose value, less her sprite's middle on screen (and less the scroll, for a byte that counts across the whole area), stays the same every time she moves. Each byte keeps a running vote: a look counts only once her sprite has moved half a step since the last one that counted (standing still says nothing, and a byte that never changes would agree), it agrees when it is within 4 pixels of its offset (a companion following her shifts her sprites' middle by 4), and it's dropped after more than one miss plus one for every 4 agreements. A byte that agreed 8 times is found, the most agreed first, then the fewest misses, then one that doesn't count the scroll, then the lowest address. A copy of the sprite table in work RAM (games build it there and have it copied in every frame, from the start of a page; found as the page holding most of the table's bytes) is never her, though it moves with her sprite best of all: games hand its places out again as sprites come and go, and in session 13 Link's Awakening's copy was someone else's once others came on screen, so her map drifted from the picture. In Link's Awakening this finds her x at FF98. From then on its value gives her spot whenever a sprite of hers is there (or the screen isn't drawn or shows no sprites), so a child beside her or a crab as the screen slides can't pull her spot away, and a step is judged by memory's pixels: 16 pixels is a step, and 17 is one step, not a ledge. After 6 looks where her sprites were drawn but nowhere near that spot, the bytes are forgotten and found again by walking: Harvest Moon's farm draws her from other bytes than its house. They're saved in `sight.json` (`found.spot`). Exploring Link's Awakening's village from the same save state, 223 walks ended free to walk against 73 before; the house explores as before, and Harvest Moon's house and farm too.

- **Which screen she's on, read from memory** (`any/memory.rs`, `Screens`; `any/mod.rs`, `Sight::settled`). Counting her steps look by look drifted: every miscount, a slide's end lost, a sprite taken for her, carried on for good. So, where her spot comes from memory, her position is read rather than counted wherever the screen stands still: between two looks on a still screen in the same place (each confirmed by a later look, not one in the same frame, still on that scroll: Link's Awakening's room number changes a moment before the screen starts to slide), she went as far as memory's pixels and the scroll say (the scroll comes round every 256 pixels, so of the steps they could mean, the one nearest the count: two slides between looks had jumped her 16 steps). In a game that slides from screen to screen, a cheat-finder vote over those looks finds the screen's own byte: one that changes by the same amount every time she slides on right (back by as much left), by another amount down, and never while she stays put. Her sprite crossing the screen against the way she went, or the scroll changing, says she slid. A copy of the camera follows slides one way only, and comes round again (Link's Awakening's is back where it was 8 screens on), so it never wins; the byte has to follow 3 slides across and 3 up or down. In Link's Awakening this finds the room number at DB54 (1 a room right, 16 down). Each screen of a place then starts at one step, kept from the first time she stood there (`Sight`'s `origins`, saved with the byte in `sight.json` as `found.screens`), and coming back puts her on it whatever went wrong in between. Explores of 600 steps from 7 Link's Awakening save states: outdoors, where the byte was found (4 of them), the rooms fit together as one map within a step 99.8-100% of looks, against 54-100% before (sessions 13 and 14's saves had 58% and 54%); the other 3 never slid enough both ways to find it and play as before. Replays of the last 20 episodes of sessions 11 to 14 (each starting from that session's saved world, so the byte is rarely found within one episode) fit within a step 86-99% of outdoor looks, against 78-96% before. A slide that doesn't scroll the screen keeps the count across it. Where the byte counts a grid of screens (one step a whole number of the other: in Link's Awakening, 16 rooms to a row), a screen's start is also worked out from the others: screens side by side are as many steps apart as most such pairs say (10 across, 8 down), and the place's screens line up where most of them say, so a screen she was put on with no slide or door (a warp, a game over) starts where it should, and one whose first visit was counted wrong is put right. Once the byte is known (it's saved), a still look puts her on her screen even before her sprite's bytes are found, from the sprite followed for her; only the steps between looks wait for memory's pixels, since a followed sprite can be somebody else's. Inside, each of Link's Awakening's houses is one screen, so the byte has nothing to say there; a dungeon may count its rooms in another byte, and snapping by one that doesn't change as she slides would put every room on the first. So in a place where the byte stood still the last time she slid on, she isn't put on her screen by it until it follows a slide there again, and her steps are read and counted as before. In session 15's replays, Link came back from a game over in the shop 15 steps off and stayed off for 25 looks; now the first still look puts him right, and outdoor looks fit within a step 97.7% (96.7% before). Played live (session 15, 23 October: Haiku playing and planning, from session 14's save with fresh maps, 40 episodes, $0.23), the outdoors stayed one place all session, three places in all.

The strategist gets a picture of the screen with every ask, since a game with no memory map has no facts: in such a game the picture is most of what it knows about where she is. Where code knows which sprite is hers (games where she walks across the screen), the pictures the models get have black corner marks with a white edge round her sprite (`pictures::png_marked`): in session 16 the models took the village boy beside Link's Awakening's shop for Link, while Link stood behind it, and every walk that episode started from the wrong place. Checkpoint pictures come from the same screen, so they're marked too.

- **Words on screen** (`text.rs`, `letters.rs`). Games draw text with tiles, one letter a tile, so once each letter's picture has a name, the words read exactly with no model. A text box is what covers her map while she can't walk: the tiles changed since she last walked, with no scroll, that together fill most of a box (a few tiles changing here and there are the map moving), less a frame round it (an edge with no blank tile). Its letters are the tiles that aren't one colour all over, and a word is letters with no blank tile between them. A box with letters the font hasn't named is kept with a picture of it, the box typed furthest only (text comes up a letter at a time), up to 8 of them; after each action the supervisor's model is shown them, told how many words each row has and how many tiles each word is, and asked to copy them out. Only a row with as many words and letters as the screen shows is taken, so a misread word names nothing, and a tile is called what most readings called it (`#` is a tile that isn't a letter, like an icon). Those boxes' words go into the episode's words, and from then on boxes read as she plays: `read_text` stops once a box's words stop changing, the tools show the words on screen, and her skills' rules can match them. The font is saved in `sight.json`, so a game's letters are named once; a replay names them at the same moment the session did (`named letters` entries). In Link's Awakening, talking to Marin named 20 letters with one Haiku call, and talking to her again read her words straight off the screen. A scene that moves a block of the map can pass for a box (Pokémon Trading Card Game's lab, as the doctor walks up): the model calls those tiles `#`, and a box of them is never asked about again.

The facts are still empty in a game with no memory map, unless she watches values herself (`ram_watch`).

**Measured on Red (21 October 2026).** On Red's opening played by script, from her bedroom to Viridian Mart (833 looks): x and y found as Red's own `wXCoord` and `wYCoord` after 9 steps, then right on all 167 steps; each of the 7 places got a name of its own (`the_look_with_no_map_agrees_with_reds`). Red's `wCurMap` is never dropped, though a byte that changes with it (the map's blocks) can name the places instead, which works as well. The maps agree with Red's own look on 1,007 steps and disagree on 20. Scenes (Oak leading her) look walkable to the look with no map, since nothing on screen says otherwise. On the test game (`test_game.rs`), three rooms with doors where everything is kept elsewhere, x in pixels, it found x, y and the room byte and drew every room right (528 steps right, none wrong).

**Played live (21 October 2026, Haiku with `VIVY_GB_LOOK=any`).** The first session, 20 episodes, got from her bedroom to Route 1 with a starter for $0.05 and 54k free tokens, but places were renamed each time a better byte turned up. After those fixes, a session of 30 episodes (22 October) went from her bedroom to Viridian City and its Mart, with one name for each of the 8 places all the way, for $0.11 and 122k free tokens. Two things went wrong: the rival's battle briefly got a place of its own until it was merged back into the lab (still to fix), and in Viridian she hopped down a ledge into a pocket and couldn't find the way out. ## Tests

`cargo test gameboy` runs everything on `test_rom.rs`'s little program, so no ROM is needed: frames, buttons reaching the game, the screen, save states playing back the same (also on a second Game Boy), a Game Boy Color game, GIFs and PNGs, typed presses, folders and names, and a panic on the emulator's thread. `test_game.rs`'s three rooms test the look with no memory map: `the_look_with_no_map_learns_a_game_it_was_never_told_about` explores them through every door and checks the bytes it found, one name per room, and the walls it drew. The second room has a ledge and floor arrows that carry her right: `ledges_and_arrows_are_learned_by_trying_them` hops the ledge once and rides the arrows once, and checks that every ledge and arrow step is then known by its look, the way goes down over the ledge in one press and around it going up, and the arrows' landing is used.

Nine more need a real game and are `#[ignore]`d. With your own ROM:

```bash
VIVY_GB_ROM=roms/red.gb cargo test --release gameboy -- --ignored --nocapture
```

While changing code, `--profile fast-test` instead of `--release` builds as fast a game, but in small pieces rebuilt incrementally: a small change rebuilds in 15 seconds to 2 minutes, depending on what it touches, instead of over 3 minutes every time (the first build takes longer).

That includes `build_the_task_states` and `actions_carry_out_tasks_by_script` from stage 3 (see "The gate") and `skills_she_writes_carry_out_battles` from stage 4; the gate's model runs skip themselves without `VIVY_GB_GATE=1`. `supervisor.rs` tests the task loop with a scripted model and a pretend game: reminders, the loop warning, the action limit. `session.rs` plays whole sessions the same way, in a pretend game of rooms in a row: checkpoints at new rooms, the ladder's warning, a rollback and its note to the strategist, going further back each time and then giving up, the caps, a model that keeps failing, every file a restart needs, the log's reports (checkpoint pictures, the warning, milestones, a blackout's line) and what the dashboard is sent; one session checks her notes against the rooms' maps and reaches milestones by area. `notes.rs` tests that old notes load, the checks (a wall, a door, a way in on the floor, notes that change by themselves), the spots it reads, milestones by area and by fact, and that the strategist can't replace a reached one. `dashboard.rs` tests the dashboard's text, while playing and after, and that a long one leaves out her notes first. `a_session_plays_red_and_its_saves_load` (ignored, needs the ROM) plays two scripted episodes of Red and checks the checkpoint and `latest` load into another Game Boy in the right place, and that a new session gets the maps back.

The stage 2 ones play Red's opening first (mashing A, picking the names RED and BLUE) and start in her bedroom: `walks_out_of_the_house_and_into_oak` walks to the stairs through steps not seen yet, out of the front door and north until Oak stops her, checking memory and the screen agree on every step and that the font reads Oak's words; `exploring_fills_in_the_bedroom` explores until every step of the room is seen; `look_and_walk_read_well_on_a_real_game` prints what `look` and `walk` would post.

`gate/other_games.rs` tries other games (all ignored, `VIVY_GB_OTHER` is the ROM): `boot_and_press_through` boots one and presses a script of buttons (`VIVY_GB_SCRIPT`, e.g. `start:120 a:60 w:300`) and saves each screen and the last state; `explore_another_game` explores from a saved state (`VIVY_GB_FROM`) and reports the places, their maps and every walk, and saves a state where she first walks in each place, to start other runs from; `steps_by_hand` takes the steps in `VIVY_GB_WALK` (`u d l r`, and `A`, `B`, `S` for Start, `F` to find her, `pr:20` to hold right for 20 frames as her own presses do) and saves a screen after each (with `VIVY_GB_SPRITES` it prints every sprite too, with `VIVY_GB_DUMP` it saves work and high RAM after each step to search offline, and `VIVY_GB_HOLDS=c606` says whether that byte is still a guess for x or y): from a session's `latest.state`, a stuck spot is replayed in under a second with no model.

Every session keeps its last 20 episodes in the game's `replays` folder: `<n>.state` (the game as the episode began) and `<n>.json` (the task, what code knew of the world then, and every action she took with what it said). `gate/replay.rs`'s `replay_episodes` plays them again with no model (`VIVY_GB_REPLAY` is one `<n>.json` or the folder, `VIVY_GB_ROM` the game): each action prints `=` when it says what it said then and `≠` with both when not, and a screen after each goes to `VIVY_GB_RESULTS`. After a fix, the episode that went wrong shows whether it's fixed, in seconds and for nothing. A test session's episodes play back exactly; the bot's keep running while she thinks, so theirs are close. Replays recorded before her whereabouts were saved start at a new place's (0, 0). Tried on 22 October 2026 on Cherry's own cartridges: in Link's Awakening, Link walks Tarin's house in exact steps, walls and pots are learned by bumping into them (a pot says "This looks pretty heavy!"), and the door is blocked by Tarin until she talks to him, as the game intends; in Harvest Moon the farmer walks his house, out of the door onto the farm and back, and the house is known again. `the_cheat_finder_finds_reds_own_addresses` and `the_look_with_no_map_agrees_with_reds` play Red's opening by script with the look with no memory map beside Red's own, and write a report (`perception` and `perception-look` in `VIVY_GB_RESULTS`): the bytes it picked against Red's addresses, where the two looks disagree on being free to walk, each place's name and its map against Red's.
