# Hunger Games

A battle royale between server members, played in plain Discord messages. The host picks the tributes (others can volunteer), and Vivy, as the Gamemaker, writes every round of what happens in the arena until one tribute is left. The arena's areas are the server's own channels.

It's one of the games in the games feature (`src/features/games/`), next to [who said it?](who-said-it.md): Discord needs one `/games` command to hold both games, so they share that feature and its gate, `[features.games]`. It borrows who said it?'s quote reader and channel rules for last words and the arena.

All 5 stages of the plan are built: the text game, last words, her memory notes about the tributes, the Gamemaker's voice, pictures, and the leaderboard.

## Playing

1. `/games hungergames` opens **the Reaping** in the channel. Whoever ran it is the host.
2. The host picks tributes from the user menu. Anyone can press **Volunteer** to enter (and again to back out). Bots can't enter. 2 to 24 tributes (`max_tributes`).
3. The host can press **🧠 Memories** to turn Vivy's memory notes about the tributes on (they're off by default; see [Memory notes](#memory-notes)) **🎨 Pictures** to have her draw a map of the arena and close-ups of the best events after every round (off by default; see [Pictures](#pictures)) and **👥 Teams** for a team Games (off by default; see [Team Games](#team-games)), then **Start**, or **Cancel**. Nobody starting within `lobby_minutes` (10) closes the Reaping.
4. The rounds follow, one message each (two or more when a round is long): **the Scatter** (everyone starts unarmed in a ring around the Octagon in the middle and runs for the zones), then days and nights, with **the Octagon** after a day now and then (see [The Octagon](#the-octagon)), and, once two sides are left, a round or two of them **wandering** the burning arena, where they may meet or not, until the fire drives them together for **the showdown** (see [The last two](#the-last-two)). Each event is its own paragraph, 💀 when someone dies in it and 🗡️ when someone gets a weapon. Rounds, cannons and the victor are plain message text, not embeds, because embeds use Discord's smaller font. Tributes show as mentions and the arena's places as channel links (`ui::mention`, `ui::link_places`, which turns each `#name` she writes into `<#id>`), so both stand out in blue, but the messages ping nobody, whatever a name or her text says. Her Gamemaker line opens the Games, each Octagon and the showdown, and is also spoken as a voice message in the Gamemaker's voice just before that round (see [Gamemaker voice](#gamemaker-voice)).
5. After each night, **the fallen of the day** lists who died since the last list, each with their **last words** (a real message they once sent, with a link to it) when one was found. Each one's kills are next to their name. When the game ends, **the last of the fallen** covers the rest, the showdown's loser among them.
6. The next round starts by itself after `auto_advance_seconds` (45). There's no Next button: Cherry turned manual advancing off so each round and its picture can go out together. Bot admins can press the host's buttons in the Reaping too.
7. At the end, **the victor** message: her line to the winner, everyone's placing with their kills and the Octagons they won, the cowards (🐔: everyone who was alive when an Octagon opened but never stepped into one), and a small line pointing to the leaderboard. Her line to the winner follows as a voice message.
8. **`/games leaderboard`** shows the server's top 10: wins, then kills, then Octagons won, then fewest games (Octagons won only show on a line when there are some), with how many games were played and the most kills anyone got in one game. With `member`, it shows one member's record instead: wins and games, kills (and the most in one game), Octagons won, best finish and average place, the most days they lived through, and their nemesis (😈, whoever killed them most, at least twice). Both are plain messages that name people without pinging them. **`/games resetleaderboard`** (bot admins only, from the `admins` list) deletes the server's saved games, so the leaderboard starts over.

A channel has one Hunger Games at a time, and a restart ends the game. Only games that reach a victor are saved for the leaderboard; cancelled and called-off games aren't.

## Settings

Under `[features.games.hunger_games]` in config.toml: `max_tributes` (24, at most 24), `lobby_minutes` (10), `auto_advance_seconds` (45, at least 5).

Each round is one model call with the job `hunger_games`: the chat provider's model at low effort, with no web or code (see [ai.md](../ai.md#jobs)). `[ai.claude.jobs.hunger_games]` changes that.

## How it works

### Who decides what

Vivy writes everything: which events happen, who meets whom, who dies and who kills them. The code only checks that what she wrote can happen, and sets the pace:

- every living tribute is in exactly one event, and nobody dead or unknown is;
- each event names its tributes in the text, and no other living tribute (the dead can be named anywhere: looted, avenged, mourned; refusing those caused most retries). A living tribute she tags or names in an event's deaths, finds or alliances but lists in no event's `tributes` is added to that event's tributes for her (`Game::fill_in_tributes`), since a forgotten victim cost a retry;
- a death is of someone in that event, and a killer is another tribute in it (or nobody, for an accident or the arena);
- a weapon find (`finds`) is by someone in that event who lives through it, with a name of 1 to 60 characters; alliances (`allies`, at least two) and walkouts (`leaves`) are by people in that event;
- in an Octagon round, `champion` is one of the entrants and lives, gets the prize (a weapon in their `finds`), and only entrants die;
- the pace (`deaths_range`): at most 2 die in the Scatter (everyone runs, so it may be 0), 1 to one fewer than the entrants in the Octagon (losers may yield instead), and an eighth to a sixth on days and nights, with at least half of that. A day or night may pass with nobody dying when the most is 1, but never two in a row. Until the last two sides at least two stay alive; then up to a whole side may fall while they wander (nobody has to), and one whole side falls in the showdown. In a simulation, 8 tributes took about 7 or 8 rounds, 12 about 10, and 24 about 16.

### The Octagon

The Octagon is the eight-sided cage in the middle of the arena, and it opens rarely so each one is a big moment (Cherry's call). `octagon_due` counts deaths: 12 or more tributes get two, once about a third and again about two thirds have fallen; 6 to 11 get one, at about a third fallen (half came too late: in a 10-player game it opened on day 4); fewer get none. It only opens with at least 4 alive in at least three sides (the last two wander instead), comes after a day (`Day 2: the Octagon`), and the night follows as usual.

Who steps in is up to the code, not Vivy: every tribute gets a hidden nerve (0 to 99) when the game starts, and each kill adds 15. `entrants` takes everyone alive with 60 or more, but never fewer than the two boldest or more than four. Vivy announces the prize in her Gamemaker line, always a legendary weapon, the deadliest in the arena, and writes one-on-one bouts until one champion is left. A loser dies or yields and limps out to boos. Everyone else sits it out. The round's message lists who stepped in (🥊) and the champion (🏆), and `Round` keeps both (`entrants`, `champion`): `Game::octagon_wins` counts a tribute's wins for the victor message and the leaderboard, and `Game::cowards` finds everyone who was alive when an Octagon began but never stepped into one. **The prize vote.** Before each Octagon, while people read the day before it, the game posts a vote with a button for each prize (`ui::prize_vote`): ⚔️ a legendary weapon, 🛡️ golden armour, or 🐺 the hunt (Cherry's idea). Anyone in the channel can vote, once, and change their vote until it closes, two thirds of `auto_advance_seconds` after it opens (a Discord countdown shows when); the message shows the count as votes come in. The most votes win, and a tie or no votes at all comes to chance. The vote message then shows what won (`ui::prize_chosen`), `Game::prize` holds it, and she writes the Octagon with it, so that round is written (and drawn) in the last third of the wait, and posted once it's ready if that takes longer (Cherry's call). `Round::reward` keeps what each champion won, and the champion line names it. A weapon must be in the champion's `finds`. Golden armour means the champion can't die in the next two rounds (`Game::armoured`; not in the showdown, which must have a winner, see [The last two](#the-last-two)). The hunt means the champion must kill someone in the next round (`Game::hunter`; that round can't be quiet). Vivy is told about each in the tributes' state and the round's brief, and the check enforces them.

Winning is meant to matter (Cherry asked for stronger Octagon wins): `Game::prizes` remembers each prize weapon, and whoever carries one, the champion or whoever loots it from them, is shown to Vivy as "an Octagon prize (wins nearly every fight)", and her rules say only an ambush, a trap, being outnumbered or the arena can take them down. With pictures on, an Octagon round is drawn zoomed in on the cage, with only those who stepped in.

Her mood isn't in the prompt, so it never changes what happens. The prompt asks for a dark, brutal show where the violence is the comedy (over-the-top, absurd kills told deadpan), with kills said plainly and about half of each round's events quiet ones (camping, foraging, building traps, alliances and betrayals, talking to the cameras), death and blood but no drawn-out torture or lingering gore (Cherry's call: the first games felt too soft, with strings and bread), nothing sexual or hateful, no jokes about anyone's real body, health, identity or private life, and no favourites.

### Weapons and alliances

The code keeps what each tribute carries and who is allied with whom, so the story stays straight from round to round (Cherry's v3 plan, in /mnt/project-files/hunger-games/brainstorm.md).

- **Weapons.** Everyone starts unarmed. Weapons are found in the arena's places, and each is themed to the channel it's found in, as creative as she likes but always able to kill (a razor-edged vinyl record in a music channel, a cast-iron skillet in a cooking one). Each tribute carries one at a time: a find replaces it. Her `finds` say who got what, and `Game::apply` keeps it. The brief shows each tribute armed or unarmed, and the dead with the weapon lying where they fell, so others can loot it. The unarmed fight with fists, rocks and traps and usually lose. In pictures, each sprite holds its current weapon, or is empty-handed.
- **Alliances.** Her `allies` team people up (joining someone brings you into their alliance) and `leaves` walks someone out. Killing an ally ends the killer's alliance by itself, so a betrayal needs no extra field. The brief lists each tribute's living allies.

### Theme

Each Games gets a light theme, not a plot (Cherry's call; the code decides the deaths and the Octagon, so a story planned from the start would keep breaking). As she writes the Scatter, Vivy picks a `theme` (`game::Theme`): a `name` for the season ("The Static Season"), a `twist` everyone knows about (a storm, a sponsor, a strange rule) and a `surprise` she means to spring once, later on. `Game::check` keeps it only in the Scatter and trims it (60 characters for the name, 300 for the others), and `Game::apply` keeps it on the game. Every later brief reminds her of all three, to colour her Gamemaker lines and some events and to spring the surprise once when it fits. The Scatter's message shows the name and the twist under its heading ("🎬 **The Static Season**: acid rain falls on #memes at noon."); the surprise stays hers until she uses it. A Scatter without a theme is fine: the game just has none.

### The shrinking arena

Once a third of the tributes have fallen, the arena starts closing in to drive the survivors together (Cherry's pick from the 2026-10-19 ideas). `Game::closed` works out which places are closed from the deaths alone: the first close as soon as a third have fallen, then an even share more with each death (rounded up), the last places in the arena's order first, until only 2 are left open with 2 alive (4 in a team Games, which ends sooner). Starting at half was too late: in a 10-player team Games the fire began on day 4 and closed only 2 of 10 places. Places only close as a day or night begins, so an Octagon keeps the arena as it was. Once two sides are left, one more place closes with each round they wander (see [The last two](#the-last-two)). Vivy's brief lists only the open places, the closed ones as walled off by fire, and, in the round where places close (`Game::closing`), that anyone there flees or the fire gets them. The round's message says so first ("🔥 The arena shrinks: walls of fire close off #a and #b."), and the map draws the zones closing that round ringed by fire. The round after, they're left off the map altogether (`looks::map`), so the zones still open have more room (Cherry's call). `Game::places` is set to the arena's size when the game starts; 0 means it never shrinks.

### The last two

There's no final showdown in the Octagon anymore: Cherry found it more fun to have the last two wander until they find each other as the fire burns closer. Once only two sides have someone alive (two tributes, or two teams), the days and nights go on, and `Game::endgame` says what each one is:

- **wandering** (`Endgame::Wander`) while more than one place is open: nothing is forced (Cherry: kills aren't needed all the time, the charm is in the moments between them). They may find each other and settle it, which ends the Games with her `victory` line, or not yet: one hunting the other, near misses, a moment that isn't a fight. `deaths_range` is 0 to a whole side, and one more place burns with each such round (`Game::closed`);
- **the showdown** (`Endgame::Showdown`) once one place is left, or after 2 rounds of wandering (`MOST_WANDERING`): they meet in the last place (or one still open), and everyone on one side dies. It gets her Gamemaker line, and golden armour doesn't count in it (the hunt doesn't count in any of the last two's rounds).

The round that ends the Games, the showdown or a wandering round where they met, is headed "⚔️ Day 5: the showdown" and keeps her `victory` line. With pictures on, it's drawn on the map (by then little more than the places still open), its `picture` must tag everyone still alive, and the victor picture follows.

Since the arena is down to two places when two are left, this is at most one round of wandering and then the showdown. An arena that never shrinks (`places` 0) goes straight to the showdown.

### Team Games

With **👥 Teams** on in the Reaping and 6 or more tributes (`FEWEST_FOR_TEAMS`), `Game::pair_up` splits the tributes into random pairs when the Games start, with one on their own when the count is odd (Cherry's pick from the 2026-10-19 ideas). With fewer, the intro says it's every tribute for themselves. The intro lists the teams (`ui::teams`). Each tribute's `partner` is kept in the game, and the rules count teams instead of tributes (`Game::sides`; in a solo Games every tribute is their own team):

- teammates never kill each other (the check refuses it), and only the bolder of a pair steps into the Octagon;
- the last two are two teams (see [The last two](#the-last-two)), and everyone left on one side dies in the showdown (one or two deaths); before it, a round can't wipe out every team but one;
- the Games end when one team is left, and a team wins together (`Game::winners`), a teammate who fell on the way included: both are 👑 in the victor message ("fell on the way" for the fallen one), both are saved as 1st (the next place is 3rd), and the victor picture shows the team's survivors side by side with everyone the team killed.

Vivy is told it's a team Games, each tribute's teammate (and whether they've fallen) is in their state, and the showdown's brief asks for a whole team to fall. Alliances between teams still form and break as usual. Round messages count the teams left too ("-# 5 tributes left (3 teams)").

### Grudges and nemeses

Every kill in a finished game is saved (`games_hunger_kills`, migration 3), so the next Games remember who killed whom in this server (Cherry's pick from the 2026-10-19 ideas). When a game starts, `stats::grudges` reads every earlier killer and victim pair among its tributes, and `Game::remember` keeps the 8 most frequent as `Grudge`s. The intro message lists the old grudges ("😈 @Moss has killed @Ash twice before."), and each round's brief tells Vivy the grudges whose two tributes are both alive, as a story hook (a taunt, a revenge hunt, a rematch), not a script: revenge can fail. When someone kills their old killer, the event gets a line under it ("😈 Revenge: @Ash killed @Moss in an earlier Games."). In `/games leaderboard member`, a member's record names their nemesis: whoever killed them most in the server, at least twice. Kills are only saved from this version on, so older games bring up no grudges.

Each kill also keeps how it happened (migration 4): the killer's weapon (what they carried coming into the event, or else what they grabbed in it; '' for bare hands), the place (the first arena place her event text names, `ui::first_place`) and whether it was in the Octagon (`Game::kills`). Revenge calls back to it: the intro says "😈 @Moss has killed @Ash once before, the last time with a pushpin spike in #memes.", the revenge line "…in an earlier Games, with a pushpin spike in #memes.", Vivy's brief "the last time with a pushpin spike" (no place, since it may not be in this arena), and the nemesis line "…who killed them 3 times, the last time with an axe" (`ui::with_weapon` adds "a" or "an" unless the name already starts with one, a capital or a possessive). Kills saved before this have no weapon, so they keep the plain wording.

### Tags instead of names

In what she writes, tributes are tags: `T1` is the first tribute. The text says `{T3} trips over a root`, and `ui.rs` fills in the name in bold when it's shown (`fill`). Names can't be misspelled or confused, and a name with Discord formatting in it stays as it is.

### One round

`writer::brief` tells her the arena's places: one per tribute but at most 8 (`MOST_PLACES`, one ring of zones around the Octagon on the map, since more were hard to read), picked at random when the game starts from every channel Vivy can see (text, announcement and forum channels where games aren't turned off; not voice or stage channels, Cherry's call), leaving out the channel the game is played in, and the same places all game. Only the names are used, so private channels can be places (Cherry: nothing in the server is really hidden); last words still come only from channels everyone can read. It also tells her each tribute's tag, name, kills or death, weapon and allies, the story so far with names filled in, what kind of round it is, and how many must die. She answers with one JSON object:

```json
{"gamemaker": "…or null", "events": [{"text": "{T1} …", "tributes": ["T1", "T2"], "deaths": [{"tribute": "T2", "by": "T1"}], "finds": [{"tribute": "T1", "weapon": "…"}], "allies": [], "leaves": [], "picture": "…or null"}], "victory": "…or null", "picture": "…or null"}
```

`parse` takes the object out of her answer (a code fence around it is fine), and `Game::check` turns it into a `Round` or a sentence saying what's wrong. A wrong round goes back to her with that sentence, as the next turn of the same conversation; after 3 tries the game is called off with a message.

### Last words

When the Games start, a task looks for each tribute's last words, 4 tributes at a time, with Discord's message search (`util::search`): their messages (`author_type` user, no links) in the channels everyone can read (the arena's channels and the one the game is played in), from `lookback_days` ago to an hour ago. One search finds how many there are (and the newest 25); then up to 3 pages at random offsets, stopping at the first with a message that fits the who-said-it rules (`min_length`, `max_length`, at least 3 words, no links) and is at most 200 characters. That's at most 4 Discord calls per tribute, no AI. Anyone the search finds nothing for (Discord may still be indexing the server) gets up to 3 random reads of 100 messages per tribute with the who-said-it quote reader. People in `exclude_users` get none. The words are put on one line. The first cannons wait for the task if it isn't done. Someone with nothing found falls without last words.

### Memory notes

Before the Scatter, if the host turned **Memories** on in the Reaping (it starts off: Cherry found the notes lame in practice), `notes::about` reads Vivy's notes about each tribute: the files in `/memories/users/<user id>/` of the server's folder, at most 6 files and 800 characters per tribute. It doesn't import the memory feature: it finds the feature that offers the `memory` chat tool and runs `view` with it as Vivy, so the memory feature's gate and rules still apply (memory off in that server or channel means no notes).

Each round's brief lists the notes of the living tributes, with the instruction to use a detail only where it makes a moment funnier or more personal, a few per round at most, never anything private, sensitive, hurtful or embarrassing (health, relationships, family, where they live, work or money troubles, anything told in confidence), and to leave them out when nothing fits (Cherry's call: fun things when there are some, nothing forced).

### Gamemaker voice

Her four or so Gamemaker lines a game (opening the Games, each Octagon, the showdown, and her words to the victor) are spoken in a voice of their own: `gamemaker` in `[features.voice.voices]`, `DucUc05HgR5M3wbY6WkG` by default, a voice Cherry made with ElevenLabs Voice Design. `voice::speak` doesn't import the voice feature: it finds the feature that offers `send_voice_message` and runs it as Vivy with `voice: "gamemaker"` and `reply: false`, so the voice message stands on its own in the channel and counts toward the voice feature's monthly limit. Her mood isn't in it.

The writer's format tells her the lines are spoken and lets her steer them with audio tags (`[booming]`, `[dramatic pause]`), each line under 300 characters. The round and victor texts show the line without the tags (`ui::unspoken`). Without an ElevenLabs key, with voice off in the channel, with the `gamemaker` voice empty, or when the month's characters are used up, the lines are text only; the round waits for the voice message (a few seconds, with the typing indicator) before it's posted.

### Pictures

When the host turns **🎨 Pictures** on in the Reaping (off by default, since they cost money), every round comes with pictures, nights included: a map and close-ups of up to 3 events (see [Close-ups](#close-ups)). Each round gets a map: a top-down 2D map like a retro strategy game, with the Octagon in the middle (an eight-sided fighting cage on a stone platform), a labelled zone for each of the arena's places, and the tributes as pixel-art sprites (a little bigger than usual, to read them) where each event happens. Cherry chose the map because it's the easiest to read (realistic and cartoon scenes came first). With each of those rounds she also writes a `picture`: every event's zone and what its tributes do there, with tags for the tributes and anyone who dies lying dead where they fell. `Game::check` turns a `picture` down when it tags nobody, and the Octagon's and the showdown's when they leave out a fighter (entrants, or everyone still alive): names instead of tags get no sprites, so the image model drew strangers in a final showdown. Every prompt is logged (`drew a Hunger Games picture`) so a picture can be checked against what she asked for. The Scatter shows everyone running out from their plates around the Octagon, and nights are dark with campfires and hiding spots. Her picture prompts use plain words ("the Octagon", "players"), never Hunger Games terms, since the image model once drew a literal cornucopia. Each map also says exactly how many players are on it (everyone alive when the round began) and that nobody else is, and zone terrain is scenery only, since the first maps filled zones with crowds, ninjas and robots. Octagon rounds are zoomed in on the cage instead of showing the whole map (`looks::cage`, style `pictures::CAGE`, no labels), so the fighters are drawn big: an Octagon round shows only those who stepped in (the champion with the prize, the losers dead or limping out), and the picture says exactly how many there are. Then the victor message gets a picture of its own, made by the bot (`looks::victor`): the winner as a large full-body figure, with everyone they killed lying dead around them. The showdown and the victor are drawn side by side. The pictures show deaths plainly, with some pixel-art blood (Cherry found knocked-out players sitting around awkward). The maps and close-ups are drawn at `medium` quality (12 to 14 cents each) and the victor at `high` (about 44 cents), whatever `[features.drawing] quality` says (Cherry's call; `pictures::draw` passes `quality` with the `game_art` job, which the drawing tool only takes from games). Games' pictures are WebP, so a round's pictures together stay well under Discord's upload limit.

**Close-ups.** On a map with many zones and players the sprites were small and hard to read, and a high-quality map cost 44 cents, so since #81 every round's map is medium, and beside it Vivy picks the 3 most interesting events (all of them when there are 3 or fewer, `game::MOST_CLOSE_UPS`, Cherry's call) and writes each a `picture` of its own: a close-up of that event alone, with tags for everyone in it. `looks::scene` zooms in on the zone the event's text names (its terrain from the looks; fire at the edge when that place closes this round; dark at night) and says exactly how many players are in it, in the `pictures::SCENE` style: the same pixel art, zoomed in so the sprites are big. In an Octagon round each bout is zoomed in on the cage instead (`looks::cage`). A close-up that tags nobody is left out rather than rewritten, and any past the first 3 are dropped (`Game::check`).

**Looks.** So the map and the tributes look the same in every picture, `looks::describe` runs once as the Games start, while she writes the Scatter: one model call (the `hunger_games` job) with every tribute's profile picture (256 px, their server one when set) and the arena's places. It describes a sprite for each tribute, inspired by their picture (bold colors, headgear, outfit, and nothing held, since weapons change during the game), and each zone's terrain, fitting its name when it can. A tribute whose picture can't be loaded gets a sprite invented from their name. `looks::map` lays the zones out around the Octagon in a fixed order (north, east, south, west, then the diagonals, then rings further out), labelled with the place's name in plain letters, and `looks::fill` puts each tribute's sprite, holding the weapon they carry once the round is played (or empty-handed). A weapon looted this round is drawn only in its new hands, not also by the body (`Game::weapons_drawn`: someone who died is drawn empty-handed when someone else in the same event found a weapon, or a living tribute carries one by the same name), and the showdown and the victor's picture show the weapons they fought with, from before the round, in place of their tag in her prompt (or "a player" when there's no sprite). The first picture waits for the looks if they aren't ready. Without the drawing tool there (drawing off in the channel, or no OpenAI key) the call isn't made and no prompts are asked for.

**Drawing.** Each map is drawn right after its round is written, in the same background task, and the map and close-ups are drawn side by side, so the round and its pictures are posted together in one message (the map first, then the close-ups in event order, on the round's last part). The Scatter's are drawn once the round is posted instead, as their own message ("🗺️ The arena as the Games begin"), since nothing waits on it and people are already reading. `pictures::draw` doesn't import the drawing feature: it finds the feature that offers `draw_picture` and runs it with the host as the asker and arguments the chat model isn't told about: the style added to the prompt (`MAP`, `CAGE`, `SCENE` or `VICTORY`), landscape, `post: false` so the picture comes back to be posted with the round, and the job `game_art`, so the pictures' cost has its own line in OpenAI's spend. The drawing feature's gate and limits apply, and the pictures count toward the host's monthly pictures (a round is about 4 pictures, so `per_user_monthly_pictures`, 20 by default, is less than one game) and the month's total. Games use OpenAI's less strict safety filter (`moderation` "low"), since the host chose a game with deaths and blood in it. A picture that fails is logged as a warning with OpenAI's reason, and the first time one is missing in a game the channel gets a small line saying why ("🗺️ No picture this time: this month's pictures are used up.", or the safety filter turned it down). When Discord refuses a round's pictures together, the text is posted on its own and each picture after it, so a bad one loses only itself.

### Pace

While people read a round, the next one is already being written (and drawn), in a task started right after posting. After `auto_advance_seconds` the game waits for it if it isn't done yet, with Discord's typing indicator showing.

## Feature hooks it uses

Through the games feature (`games/mod.rs`): the `hungergames` and `leaderboard` subcommands of `/games`, `on_component` for IDs starting with `games:hunger:`, and the games feature's migrations (owner `games`). The leaderboard is `/games leaderboard`, not `/games hungergames leaderboard`: Discord allows only two levels under a command, and making `hungergames` a group would turn starting a game into `/games hungergames start`.

## Tables

- `games_hunger_games`: one row per finished game: server, channel, winner, how many rounds, when.
- `games_hunger_tributes`: one row per tribute in it: place (1 is the victor), kills, Octagons won (`octagons`, added by migration 2), and days lived through (the highest day of the rounds before the one they died in, so the Scatter is 0, dying on day 2 is 1 and on night 2 is 2). Deleting a game deletes its rows.
- `games_hunger_kills` (migration 3): one row per kill in a finished game: the killer and the victim, for grudges and nemeses. Migration 4 adds `weapon` (NULL for kills saved before it, '' for bare hands), `place_id` and `octagon`. Deleting a game deletes its rows.

## Background loops

None: a task per running game.
